# Segment-level FPR audit by order-value bucket

Generated by `python -m src.segment_audit`, which writes
`segment_fpr_{full,testbed}.{json,png}`. Buckets are quartiles of
`avg_order_value_usd` on the 12,000-row test set (same four cut points for
both tracks), computed against the routed action from the exact
cost-calibrated decision layer `src/evaluate.py` and `src/infer.py` use
(`build_cost_matrix` + `expected_cost_decision`, loss-neutral 1:1 posture),
applied to `runs/model_{track}_proba.npy` / `_ytest.npy`.

Two rates are reported per bucket, among legitimate customers only:

- **`hard_block_fpr`** — the literal false-positive rate the checklist item
  names: a legitimate customer wrongly hard-blocked.
- **`soft_friction_rate`** — a legitimate customer given friction. Included
  because `docs/LEAKAGE_FINDING.md`'s Day 4 finding is that hard-block is
  nearly inert on this data (0.00% false-block on `full`, 0.00–0.23% on
  `testbed` across the whole posture sweep) and approve↔soft-friction is the
  axis that actually moves. Auditing only `hard_block_fpr` would report
  ~0% in every bucket on both tracks and miss the one place segment
  concentration could plausibly show up.

## `full` track

| bucket | n legitimate | hard_block_fpr | soft_friction_rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (lowest, \$15–\$93) | 2,336 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| Q2 (\$93–\$164) | 2,104 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| Q3 (\$164–\$248) | 2,356 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| Q4 (highest, \$248–\$800) | 1,549 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |

Exactly flat, exactly zero, in every bucket. This is the same degenerate
sweep already documented in `docs/LEAKAGE_FINDING.md` — on `full`, ~0.1% of
rows sit near any decision boundary at all, so there is no room for the
policy to concentrate false blocks anywhere, on any segment. Not a new
finding; consistent with the one already on record.

## `testbed` track

| bucket | n legitimate | hard_block_fpr | soft_friction_rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (lowest, \$15–\$93) | 2,336 | 0.0004 | **0.1040** |
| Q2 (\$93–\$164) | 2,104 | 0.0000 | **0.1231** |
| Q3 (\$164–\$248) | 2,356 | 0.0013 | 0.0730 |
| Q4 (highest, \$248–\$800) | 1,549 | 0.0000 | **0.0504** |

`hard_block_fpr` stays at noise level (0–3 rows per bucket out of ~2,000–2,300)
on `testbed` too — consistent with the Day 4 finding that the hard-block
boundary has almost nothing to act on.

`soft_friction_rate` is where the real signal is, and it **is** concentrated:
Q2 gets friction 2.44× as often as Q4 (12.31% vs. 5.04%), and the pattern is
monotonic — friction rate falls as order value rises. Flagged by this
module's own threshold (ratio ≥ 2.0×).

**Read the direction before reading it as a problem.** The concentration
runs the opposite way a merchant would worry about: **low-value legitimate
customers get frictioned more, not high-value ones.** A policy that
concentrated false friction on its highest-value customers would be actively
working against retention — the exact failure mode `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`
§1 opens with ("quietly blocking or fee-ing legitimate high-value
customers"). This policy does the reverse. That is a defensible
*direction* for a merchant to prefer, but it is not something to wave past
either: a 2.44× gap means the model's soft-friction boundary correlates with
order value, not just with abuse risk, on `testbed`'s feature set — and
`avg_order_value_usd` is itself a top-3 SHAP driver for Legitimate, Policy
Abuser, and Wardrobing alike (`shap_interpretation.md`), so this concentration
and that SHAP finding are almost certainly the same underlying effect
described two ways, not two independent findings.

## Caveat

Computed against a loss-neutral (1:1) posture only. `full`'s sweep is
already known to be flat across every posture in `docs/LEAKAGE_FINDING.md`,
so the result there would not change. `testbed`'s posture sensitivity is
already characterized in `runs/evaluation_testbed.json`
(`friction_curve_span`); this audit was not re-run across every posture, and
a full posture × segment sweep is flagged as a natural extension rather than
claimed here.
